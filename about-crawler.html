About the accessibility-map crawler

If you run a website and saw accessibility-map in your server logs, this page explains who we are and how to stop us.

Who we are

A research project at Miami University that collects publicly available accessibility information (for example: step-free entrances, elevators, accessible restrooms and parking, transit access, events) to help wheelchair users and people who are blind or have low vision plan trips. The project code is open: github.com/zenaism/accessibility-map.

What the crawler does

  • Reads only public pages and documents. It never logs in, never fills in forms, and never gets around blocks or CAPTCHAs.
  • Obeys your robots.txt, including Crawl-delay, and waits between requests. It slows down or stops when your server answers with 429 or 503.
  • Checks your terms of use pages. If they forbid automated access, we stop and do not collect from your site unless you give permission.
  • Visits a small number of pages per site, starting with accessibility, visit and contact pages.

What we keep

  • Short factual statements about accessibility (for example "the main entrance is step-free"), each with a quotation, a link back to your page and the date we read it.
  • Personal email addresses and personal phone numbers are removed. Business contact details that you publish are kept as facts about the place.
  • We do not publish copies of your pages.

How to stop us or correct a fact

Add this to your robots.txt and we will not visit your site:

User-agent: accessibility-map
Disallow: /

To ask us to remove data we already hold about your site, or to correct a fact, open an issue on GitHub and name the site. We will stop crawling it and remove its data.
